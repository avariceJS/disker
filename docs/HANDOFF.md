## Current product

Disker is a native SwiftUI macOS app with Overview, Applications, Folders, Duplicates, History, RAM, and About. It separates macOS-reported volume capacity from selected-path logical bytes and allocated-byte estimates, shows coverage/freshness, and retains bounded local observations. Complete same-path comparisons drive folder, directory, extension, large-file, candidate, and capacity trends. Overview retains the largest unrecovered available-space drop of at least 100 MiB in bounded history and calls out when no selected-folder scan explains it; capacity is never attributed to folders without aligned complete same-path evidence. When the latest selected-root scans are partial, this callout shows their measured-size estimates, exclusions, and a clear warning that current size is not growth. Application footprint review uses explicit selection and evidence-scoped associations. Duplicate verification reads selected candidate contents only after separate in-app consent and produces a dry-run after the user picks a keeper. No deletion action exists.

Developer-artifact review shows measured paths, name/Git-marker evidence, risks and consequences, logical and allocated estimates, coverage, overlaps, and complete-history deltas. It now includes up to three read-only investigation prompts: positive complete same-path growth first, then a complete candidate at least 100 MiB allocated. Formatted sizes, confidence, coverage, overlap, and a link to detail are provided; reclaimable space remains unknown. Partial roots suppress prompts and partial subtrees are excluded. This is not general cleanup discovery or a deletion plan. RAM uses snapshots and cautious sampled signals; leak diagnosis and a background service are not implemented.

Folders detail also includes a bounded review of files with at least 100 MiB allocated estimate and a filesystem modification timestamp at least 180 days old. It requires complete coverage and an untruncated largest-file inventory. The UI exposes exact path, logical and allocated sizes, date/age, and Finder reveal while explaining that old modification time does not mean unused and reclaimable space is unknown. The metadata review does not read contents or offer cleanup.

Full file-change tracking now runs alongside the capped dashboard summaries. A complete selected-root scan records every regular-file path in the explicit no-follow scope (hard links count once at their deterministic path), then stores a separate local baseline and the latest exact-path comparison. The next complete scan reports added, resized, and no-longer-present paths, net logical delta, net allocated-byte estimate, and up to 100 changed paths. It is bounded to 500,000 entries per root, 750,000 total, 50 roots, and a 128 MiB private binary plist. Partial, cancelled, concurrent-change, inaccessible, special-file, volume-boundary, accounting-overflow, or capped inventories do not replace a baseline. Symlink targets remain excluded and named in the UI. First scan creates a baseline only; no earlier growth can be recovered. This identifies net on-disk differences between scans, not short-lived activity or causal ownership. Event-based and background tracking remain unimplemented.

It also compares large file paths with the immediately prior complete inventory of the same selected root and volume, but only when both bounded summaries are untruncated and the current record is latest. Rows say “Not present in prior complete inventory”; they do not claim the file was created, is unused, belongs to a project, or is reclaimable. A newer partial/cancelled observation suppresses results.

The app-removal review now shows a measured selected-scope subtotal only for disjoint, explicitly selected scopes whose latest observations are complete, uncancelled, non-overflowed, nonnegative, and safely addable. Shared or multi-owner scopes are excluded. Any other missing or unsafe scope withholds the subtotal with a path-specific reason. This is a subtotal of selected measurements, not exclusive app ownership, exact SSD use, or reclaimable space. A second read-only disposition now makes each path's cleanup-review status and evidence visible; macOS-managed roots are excluded, unknown data remains inspect-only, and no row is presented as safe to delete.

If a selected app bundle path is unavailable, the owner can explicitly check the exact saved associated directory paths. A descriptor-based walk uses no-follow metadata operations for every path component, rejects ancestor and final symlinks, and rejects raw `.`/`..` before normalization. Results distinguish available directories from missing/unreachable, access-unknown, non-directory, symlink, and invalid/stale/mismatched bookmark cases. It does not enumerate descendants, read file contents, or refresh bookmarks. Association and bookmark changes invalidate in-flight checks. This supports investigation only; it does not confirm uninstallation or safe cleanup.

The bundle-path check uses the same typed statuses. An access or bookmark failure is shown as unconfirmed presence rather than as a missing app. The associated-folder prompt likewise says only that the bundle path could not be confirmed; it does not call this an uninstall finding.
